The idea

Real ground truth, fake weather

Simulators give exact ground truth but look synthetic, so perception that passes in a game engine fails on real rock. Real photos look right but carry no ground truth and only cover conditions we happened to drive through. Sim Genie combines both: a simulated world that knows exactly where every rock is, repainted frame-by-frame into photoreal Mars conditions by a live generative video model - so perception is tested against the rare, dangerous cases a rover actually has to survive: low sun on loose regolith, dust washing out contrast, a shadowed crater rim.

More detail

For anyone who wants the internals

Why not just use Gazebo, or real photos?

Gazebo cannot make photorealistic dust, so it can't tell you whether perception survives it. Real Mars photography has no ground truth and only covers the conditions someone happened to capture. Sim Genie's answer is to keep Unity's exact, known geometry and let a live video model handle appearance - so the hard, rare conditions can be manufactured on demand and still scored against a real answer key.

What does perception actually see?

Only the repainted camera feed - never the label camera. If perception could see the ground truth, the whole experiment would be measuring nothing. A separate scorer, which never touches perception, is the only thing comparing predictions against Unity's answer key.

What's on the control panel?
  • Three live panes: Unity's render, X2's repaint, and the segmentation overlay.
  • A reserved space to dock the Unity Play-mode window alongside them.
  • A condition box that publishes straight to the rig - type a prompt, or load the full catalogue.
  • Director and bridge status, so you can see what condition is running and whether the session is live.
How is this run, end to end?

./rig is the single entry point - install, build, test, and run, with an offline mode that proves the whole loop with no ROS, no Unity and no credits spent. See README.md in this repo for the full walkthrough.
